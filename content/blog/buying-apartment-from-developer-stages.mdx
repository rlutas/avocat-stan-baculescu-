Law no. 207/2025 (the "Nordis Law") establishes a clear sequence of stages for purchasing a property under construction. It is essential that every buyer understands the difference between them, because confusing the reservation with the promise of sale is one of the most frequent sources of disputes.

<KeyPoints>
- A reservation is not a promise of sale and does not produce the same legal effects
- A reservation is valid for a maximum of 60 days, and the amount paid cannot exceed 5% of the price
- The promise of sale must be concluded in authentic form, before a notary, and recorded in the Land Register
- The sale contract is concluded upon completion of construction and effectively transfers ownership
</KeyPoints>

## Stage 1 — The reservation

It can be concluded as early as the project phase, as soon as the developer holds the building permit. The reservation is not a promise of sale and does not produce the same legal effects — it is only a temporary arrangement, valid for a maximum of 60 days, for which no more than 5% of the price may be paid.

<Callout type="warning" title="Beware of confusing reservation with promise of sale">
The reservation is a temporary arrangement: **a maximum of 60 days** and **no more than 5% of the price**. Confusing the reservation with the promise of sale is one of the most frequent sources of disputes.
</Callout>

## Stage 2 — The promise of sale

It can only be concluded once additional conditions are met: the building permit is recorded in the Land Register, the pre-apartmentation has been carried out, and the property has its own separately opened land register entry. Unlike the reservation, the promise of sale must be concluded in authentic form, before a notary, who is also obliged to record it in the Land Register.

## Stage 3 — The sale contract

It is concluded upon completion of construction, also in authentic form, effectively transferring the right of ownership.

## Why the contractual clauses matter

Complying with the legal stages does not, however, replace a careful analysis of the contents of the contracts. Completion deadlines, late-delivery penalties, termination conditions and payment terms must be examined in detail. Legal review at every step — from the reservation through to the final contract — ensures both the compliance of the documents and a genuine balance of the obligations assumed by the parties.
