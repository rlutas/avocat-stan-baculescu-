## Overview of Divorce in Romania

Divorce in Romania is governed by the **Codul Civil** (Civil Code), specifically **art. 373-382**, which establish the legal grounds and procedures for dissolving a marriage. Romanian law offers multiple pathways for obtaining a divorce, ranging from a streamlined administrative procedure for couples who agree to separate amicably, to a full judicial process for contested cases. Understanding which procedure applies to your situation is essential for managing expectations regarding timelines, costs, and outcomes.

The legal framework aims to balance two principles: the freedom of spouses to end a marriage that is no longer functional, and the protection of vulnerable parties -- particularly minor children -- who may be affected by the dissolution.

## Legal Grounds for Divorce

Under **art. 373 of the Civil Code**, a divorce can be granted on the following grounds:

### 1. Mutual Consent of the Spouses (art. 373 lit. a)

This is the most straightforward ground for divorce. Both spouses agree that the marriage has irretrievably broken down and jointly request its dissolution. There is no requirement to prove fault or to demonstrate specific reasons for the divorce. This ground opens the door to the simplified administrative divorce procedures (notarial or civil registrar), as well as a streamlined court procedure.

### 2. Serious Grounds Rendering Continuation Impossible (art. 373 lit. b)

One spouse may request a divorce by demonstrating that there are serious grounds -- such as domestic violence, infidelity, substance abuse, or other conduct -- that make the continuation of the marriage impossible. The requesting spouse must prove these grounds before the court.

### 3. Deterioration of the Marriage Relationship (art. 373 lit. c)

A divorce may be granted at the request of one spouse if the marital relationship has seriously deteriorated and there is no reasonable prospect of reconciliation. This is a broader ground that does not require proving specific acts of fault.

### 4. De Facto Separation for at Least Two Years (art. 373 lit. d)

If the spouses have been living separately for a continuous period of at least two years, either spouse may request a divorce. This ground acknowledges the reality that a marriage may have effectively ended long before the legal dissolution is sought.

## Divorce by Mutual Consent: Administrative Procedures

Romanian law provides two administrative (non-judicial) procedures for divorce by mutual consent, which are significantly faster and less costly than court proceedings.

### Notarial Divorce (art. 375 para. 1 Civil Code)

The **divorrul pe cale notariala** (notarial divorce) is the most expedient procedure available. It can be used when:

- Both spouses agree to the divorce.
- Both spouses appear personally before the notary public.
- The spouses have agreed on all ancillary matters, including the use of the family name after divorce.

**If there are minor children**, notarial divorce is still possible, but **only if** the spouses have reached a complete agreement on parental authority, the children's residence, visitation rights, and child support. The notary must obtain an opinion from the **autoritatea tutelara** (guardianship authority) confirming that the agreement is in the best interest of the children.

**The procedure** is as follows:

1. Both spouses appear before a notary public, either in the jurisdiction where the marriage was registered or where either spouse has their last common domicile.
2. The notary records the request and grants a mandatory **30-day reflection period** (termen de gandire).
3. After the reflection period, both spouses must return to the notary to confirm their decision.
4. If both spouses confirm, the notary issues the **certificat de divort** (divorce certificate).

**Costs**: Notarial fees for divorce typically range from 500 to 1,500 RON, depending on the complexity of the case and whether ancillary matters (such as property division) are also resolved.

**Timeline**: The entire procedure takes approximately 30-45 days.

### Civil Registrar Divorce (art. 375 para. 2 Civil Code)

The **divorrul la ofiterul de stare civila** (divorce through the civil registrar) is available when:

- Both spouses consent to the divorce.
- There are **no minor children** born from the marriage, adopted, or placed in the care of the spouses.

The procedure is similar to the notarial divorce: the spouses appear before the civil registrar at the place where the marriage was registered or where either spouse has their domicile, a 30-day reflection period is observed, and if both spouses confirm their decision, the divorce certificate is issued.

**Costs**: The administrative fee for divorce at the civil registrar is established by **Legea nr. 117/2014** and is currently 500 RON.

## Court Divorce: Detailed Procedure

When the administrative procedures are not available -- because the spouses do not agree, because there are unresolved disputes concerning children or property, or because one spouse seeks a fault-based divorce -- the dissolution must be obtained through the courts.

### Jurisdiction and Competent Court

Divorce proceedings are heard by the **judecatorie** (district court), specifically the court in the jurisdiction of the last common domicile of the spouses. If the spouses have not had a common domicile, or if neither spouse still resides in that jurisdiction, the competent court is the one where the defendant has their domicile (**art. 914 of the Codul de Procedura Civila** -- Civil Procedure Code).

### Filing the Divorce Petition

The divorce petition (**cererea de divort**) must include, according to **art. 916 of the Civil Procedure Code**:

- The identity of both spouses
- The date and place of the marriage
- The names and dates of birth of minor children
- The grounds for divorce
- The petitioner's requests regarding child custody, child support, property division, and the use of the family name after divorce

The petition must be accompanied by a copy of the marriage certificate and birth certificates of the minor children.

### Court Procedure (art. 914-935 Civil Procedure Code)

The court procedure follows specific rules designed to protect both spouses and any minor children:

1. **Personal appearance**: The court will summon both spouses to appear in person. While representation by a lawyer is permitted, the judge may require the personal appearance of the parties, especially for the reconciliation attempt.
2. **Mandatory reconciliation attempt**: The court is required by law to attempt to reconcile the spouses. In practice, this occurs at the first hearing, where the judge will speak with both parties and encourage them to reconsider.
3. **Evidence and hearings**: If reconciliation fails, the court proceeds to hear evidence. This may include witness testimony, documentary evidence, and expert reports (particularly regarding the children's best interests).
4. **Judgment**: The court issues a judgment dissolving the marriage and ruling on all ancillary matters.

**Timeline**: A contested court divorce in Romania typically takes between **6 months and 2 years**, depending on the complexity of the issues, the number of hearings, and the court's caseload.

**Costs**: The court fee for a divorce petition is 200 RON for mutual consent divorces and varies for contested cases. Attorney's fees vary depending on the complexity of the case.

## Child Custody (art. 396-403 Civil Code)

The question of child custody -- known as **exercitarea autoritatii parintesti** (exercise of parental authority) in Romanian law -- is one of the most sensitive aspects of any divorce involving minor children. Romanian law establishes the following framework:

### Joint Parental Authority (art. 397 Civil Code)

The default rule under Romanian law is that parental authority is exercised **jointly** by both parents after divorce. This means both parents retain equal rights and obligations regarding the child's upbringing, education, health, and well-being, regardless of which parent the child lives with.

### Sole Parental Authority (art. 398 Civil Code)

The court may grant sole parental authority to one parent only in exceptional circumstances, where there are **serious grounds** (such as violence, abuse, neglect, or addiction) that make joint exercise contrary to the child's best interests. This is not the norm and requires substantial evidence.

### Child's Residence (art. 400 Civil Code)

The court will establish the child's primary residence with one parent. In making this determination, the court considers the child's age, the parent's ability to provide stable living conditions, the child's attachment to each parent, and the opinion of the child (if the child has reached the age of 10, their opinion is mandatory to consider under **art. 264 para. 1 Civil Code**).

### Visitation Rights (art. 401 Civil Code)

The parent who does not have the child's primary residence is entitled to maintain personal relationships with the child through a visitation schedule established by the court. The court sets the frequency, duration, and conditions of visits, taking into account the child's best interests.

## Child Support (art. 499-534 Civil Code)

Both parents are obligated to provide financial support for their minor children, proportional to their means and to the child's needs. Under Romanian law, child support (**pensie de intretinere**) is calculated as a percentage of the obligated parent's net monthly income:

- **1/4 (25%)** of net income for one child
- **1/3 (approximately 33%)** of net income for two children
- **1/2 (50%)** of net income for three or more children

These percentages are established by **art. 529 of the Civil Code**. The court may adjust the amount based on the specific needs of the child and the paying parent's financial situation. The support obligation continues until the child reaches the age of 18, or until 26 if the child is pursuing full-time education (**art. 499 para. 3 Civil Code**).

If the obligated parent's income cannot be determined (for example, if they work in the informal economy), the court will establish a fixed amount based on the national minimum wage.

Non-payment of child support is both a civil matter (subject to enforcement proceedings) and a criminal offense under **art. 378 of the Codul Penal** (Criminal Code), punishable by imprisonment from 1 to 5 years or a fine.

## Property Division (art. 339-355 Civil Code)

The division of marital property is often the most contentious aspect of a divorce. Romanian law distinguishes between two categories of property:

### Common Property (Bunuri Comune -- art. 339 Civil Code)

Assets acquired by either spouse during the marriage are presumed to be **common property** (belonging to both spouses in equal shares), unless proven otherwise. This includes real estate, vehicles, bank accounts, investments, and business interests acquired during the marriage.

### Personal Property (Bunuri Proprii -- art. 340 Civil Code)

Certain assets remain the personal property of each spouse, including:

- Assets owned before the marriage
- Assets received by inheritance or donation during the marriage (unless the donor/testator specified otherwise)
- Personal-use items
- Assets acquired with funds that are themselves personal property

### Division Procedure

Property division can be accomplished either by mutual agreement (through a notarized partition agreement) or through the courts. The default rule is that each spouse is entitled to a **50% share** of common property, but the court may adjust the shares based on each spouse's contribution to the acquisition, preservation, and improvement of the common assets (**art. 357 Civil Code**).

## Compensatory Benefits (art. 390-395 Civil Code)

Romanian law introduced the concept of **prestatia compensatorie** (compensatory benefit) to address the economic imbalance that divorce can create between the spouses. Under **art. 390 of the Civil Code**, if the divorce causes a significant deterioration in the living conditions of one spouse, that spouse may request a compensatory benefit from the other.

Key conditions for obtaining a compensatory benefit:

- The marriage lasted at least **20 years**.
- The requesting spouse did not initiate the divorce and was not at fault.
- There is a significant disparity between the living conditions of the two spouses after the divorce.

The compensatory benefit can take the form of periodic payments (a monthly amount) or a lump sum. It is distinct from child support and property division and serves to compensate the spouse who sacrificed career or earning opportunities for the benefit of the marriage.

## Required Documents

To initiate divorce proceedings, whether administrative or judicial, you will need to prepare the following documents:

- **Marriage certificate** (original and copies)
- **Identity documents** of both spouses (carte de identitate or passport)
- **Birth certificates** of minor children (if applicable)
- **Proof of domicile** for both spouses
- **Property documentation** -- title deeds, vehicle registration documents, bank statements, and any other records related to common property (if property division is sought)
- **Income documentation** -- salary certificates, tax returns, or other proof of income (relevant for child support calculations)
- **Pre-nuptial agreement** (if one exists)
- **Agreement between spouses** (if the divorce is by mutual consent, a written agreement covering all ancillary matters)

## Estimated Costs

The total cost of a divorce in Romania varies significantly depending on the chosen procedure and the complexity of the case:

- **Civil registrar divorce**: approximately 500 RON in administrative fees, plus optional legal assistance.
- **Notarial divorce**: approximately 500-1,500 RON in notarial fees.
- **Court divorce by mutual consent**: approximately 200 RON court fee, plus attorney's fees.
- **Contested court divorce**: court fees vary depending on the claims (property division claims incur additional proportional fees), plus attorney's fees which depend on the complexity and duration of the case.
