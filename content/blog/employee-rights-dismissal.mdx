## The Legal Framework for Dismissal in Romania

Employment relationships in Romania are governed primarily by the **Codul Muncii** (Labor Code), enacted through **Legea nr. 53/2003** (Law no. 53/2003), with subsequent amendments. The Labor Code establishes a comprehensive framework of employee protections that is notably more favorable to workers than the labor legislation of many other jurisdictions. Understanding these protections is essential for any employee facing the prospect of dismissal.

Under Romanian law, the employment contract is considered a protected legal relationship. An employer cannot terminate an individual employment contract at will. Every dismissal must be grounded in one of the specific legal bases provided by the Labor Code, must follow a strictly defined procedure, and must be documented in a written dismissal decision. Failure to comply with any of these requirements renders the dismissal unlawful and subject to annulment by the courts.

## Types of Dismissal

The Labor Code distinguishes between two broad categories of dismissal: dismissal for reasons related to the employee (**concediere din motive care tin de persoana salariatului**) and dismissal for reasons unrelated to the employee (**concediere din motive care nu tin de persoana salariatului**).

### Dismissal for Reasons Related to the Employee

Under **art. 61 of the Labor Code**, an employer may dismiss an employee for the following reasons:

#### a) Disciplinary Dismissal (art. 61 lit. a)

This is the most severe disciplinary sanction available to an employer and may be applied when the employee commits a **serious disciplinary offense** (abatere disciplinara grava) or repeated disciplinary offenses that make the continuation of the employment relationship impossible.

The disciplinary procedure is governed in detail by **art. 247-252 of the Labor Code** and imposes strict requirements on the employer:

1. **Written notice**: The employee must be summoned in writing to a disciplinary hearing (**cercetare disciplinara prealabila**), with at least 5 working days' notice. The notice must specify the alleged offense, the date, time, and location of the hearing.
2. **Right to defense**: During the disciplinary hearing, the employee has the right to present their defense, to provide evidence, and to be assisted by a lawyer or a union representative (**art. 251 para. 4**).
3. **Proportionality**: The disciplinary sanction must be proportional to the severity of the offense. The employer must consider the circumstances of the act, the employee's prior conduct, and any mitigating factors (**art. 250**).
4. **Written decision**: The dismissal decision must be issued in writing within **30 calendar days** from the date the employer became aware of the offense, but no later than **6 months** from the date the offense was committed (**art. 252 para. 1**).
5. **Content requirements**: The dismissal decision must contain, under penalty of absolute nullity (**art. 252 para. 2**):
   - A description of the disciplinary offense
   - The legal and factual basis for the dismissal
   - The evidence supporting the decision
   - The disciplinary sanctions previously considered and why they were rejected
   - The deadline and court for contesting the decision

**Failure to conduct the mandatory disciplinary hearing** is one of the most common procedural defects and will result in the automatic annulment of the dismissal by the court, regardless of whether the employee actually committed the alleged offense.

#### b) Preventive Detention or House Arrest (art. 61 lit. b)

The employer may dismiss an employee who has been placed in preventive detention or under house arrest for a period exceeding 30 days.

#### c) Physical or Mental Incapacity (art. 61 lit. c)

Dismissal is permitted when the competent medical authorities issue a certificate establishing that the employee suffers from a physical or mental incapacity that prevents them from fulfilling the duties of their position.

#### d) Professional Incompetence (art. 61 lit. d)

An employer may dismiss an employee for **necorespundere profesionala** (professional incompetence) -- that is, when the employee does not meet the professional performance standards required for their position. However, this ground is subject to strict conditions:

- Professional incompetence must be established through a prior **performance evaluation procedure** that is provided for in the applicable collective labor agreement or internal regulations.
- The employee must be given a reasonable opportunity to improve their performance before dismissal is considered.
- The evaluation criteria must be objective, transparent, and communicated to the employee in advance.

In practice, dismissals on this ground are frequently overturned by courts because employers fail to demonstrate that the evaluation process was fair, objective, and properly documented.

### Dismissal for Reasons Unrelated to the Employee

This category covers situations where the employer eliminates one or more positions due to economic, technological, or organizational restructuring. The position must be **effectively and genuinely eliminated** -- it cannot simply be renamed or transferred to another employee.

#### Individual Dismissal (art. 65)

An individual dismissal for reasons unrelated to the employee must meet the following conditions under **art. 65 of the Labor Code**:

- The position must be effectively suppressed from the organizational chart.
- The dismissal must be based on a real, serious cause (**cauza reala si serioasa**).
- The employer must issue a written dismissal decision containing the specific grounds and the factual circumstances justifying the position elimination.

#### Collective Dismissal (art. 68-72)

A **concediere colectiva** (collective dismissal) occurs when the employer dismisses, within a 30-day period, a certain number of employees based on reasons unrelated to their person:

- At least **10 employees** in companies with 21-99 employees
- At least **10% of employees** in companies with 100-299 employees
- At least **30 employees** in companies with 300 or more employees

The collective dismissal procedure is more complex and involves additional requirements under **art. 69-72**:

1. **Consultation**: The employer must initiate consultations with the trade union or employee representatives at least 30 days before the planned dismissals.
2. **Notification**: The employer must notify the **Inspectoratul Teritorial de Munca** (Territorial Labor Inspectorate) and the **Agentia Judeteana pentru Ocuparea Fortei de Munca** (County Employment Agency) at least 30 days before the dismissals take effect.
3. **Selection criteria**: When multiple employees hold similar positions and only some must be dismissed, the employer must apply objective selection criteria (typically established in the collective labor agreement), which may include professional performance, seniority, family situation, and other factors.
4. **Priority re-employment**: Employees who were collectively dismissed have priority for re-employment if the employer creates new positions within 45 calendar days after the dismissal, provided the positions match the employees' qualifications (**art. 72**).

## Notice Period Rights (art. 75 Labor Code)

Employees who are dismissed (with certain exceptions) are entitled to a **minimum notice period of 20 working days** (preaviz), during which they continue to work and receive their salary. The purpose of the notice period is to give the employee time to seek alternative employment.

Key rules regarding the notice period:

- The notice period begins on the day the employee is notified of the dismissal decision.
- During the notice period, the employee is entitled to be absent from work for at least **4 hours per day** to seek new employment, without any reduction in salary (**art. 75 para. 1**).
- Individual or collective labor agreements may provide for longer notice periods.
- The notice period is **not required** in cases of disciplinary dismissal (**art. 61 lit. a**) or dismissal due to preventive detention (**art. 61 lit. b**).

## Dismissal Prohibitions (art. 60 Labor Code)

Romanian law establishes a series of situations in which dismissal is **strictly prohibited**, regardless of the employer's reasons. Under **art. 60 of the Labor Code**, an employer may not dismiss an employee during:

- **Temporary incapacity for work** (medical leave), as established by a medical certificate.
- **Quarantine** periods.
- **Pregnancy**, provided the employer was informed of the pregnancy before issuing the dismissal decision.
- **Maternity leave** and **risk maternity leave**.
- **Parental leave** for raising a child up to the age of 2 (or 3, in the case of a child with a disability).
- **Leave for caring for a sick child** up to the age of 7 (or 18 for a child with a disability).
- **Annual leave** periods.
- **Exercising a union function**, to the extent that the dismissal is based on the employee's union activity.
- **Military service** or other situations provided by law.

Any dismissal decision issued in violation of these prohibitions is **absolutely null** and will be annulled by the court upon the employee's request.

Additionally, **Legea nr. 202/2002 privind egalitatea de sanse si de tratament intre femei si barbati** (Law no. 202/2002 on equal opportunities between women and men) and **Ordonanta Guvernului nr. 137/2000 privind prevenirea si sanctionarea tuturor formelor de discriminare** (Government Ordinance no. 137/2000 on preventing and sanctioning all forms of discrimination) prohibit discriminatory dismissals based on gender, age, ethnicity, religion, disability, sexual orientation, or any other protected characteristic.

## Contesting a Dismissal (art. 252 para. 5 Labor Code)

If you believe your dismissal was unlawful, you have the right to contest it before the competent court. The key procedural rules are:

### Filing Deadline

Under **art. 252 para. 5 of the Labor Code**, the employee must file the contest (**contestatie**) within **45 calendar days** from the date they were notified of the dismissal decision. This deadline is strictly enforced -- if the employee fails to file within this period, they lose the right to challenge the dismissal, regardless of how egregious the procedural violations may have been.

### Competent Court

The contest is filed with the **tribunal** (county-level court), specifically the labor and social insurance section (**sectia de litigii de munca si asigurari sociale**), in the jurisdiction where the employee has their domicile or workplace (**art. 269 Labor Code**).

### Exemption from Court Fees

One of the most important employee protections in Romanian labor law is that **all labor disputes initiated by employees are exempt from court fees** (**art. 270 Labor Code**). This means that an employee can challenge an unlawful dismissal without any financial barrier, which is a significant advantage compared to other areas of litigation.

### Burden of Proof

In labor disputes, the burden of proof regarding the legality and factual basis of the dismissal rests with the **employer** (**art. 272 Labor Code**). This is a notable reversal of the general civil law principle, where the claimant typically bears the burden of proof. The employer must demonstrate that the dismissal was based on valid grounds, that the correct procedure was followed, and that all legal requirements were met.

## Court Remedies (art. 80 Labor Code)

If the court finds that the dismissal was unlawful, **art. 80 of the Labor Code** provides the following remedies:

### 1. Annulment of the Dismissal Decision

The court will declare the dismissal decision null and void (**nulitatea absoluta a deciziei de concediere**).

### 2. Reinstatement

The employee is entitled to be reinstated in their previous position, with the same rights and working conditions they had before the unlawful dismissal. The employer cannot refuse to comply with a court order for reinstatement.

### 3. Payment of Compensatory Salary Rights

The employer must pay the employee an amount equal to the **indexed, increased, and updated salaries** and all other rights the employee would have been entitled to receive from the date of the dismissal until the date of actual reinstatement (**art. 80 para. 1**). This can represent a very substantial sum if the litigation lasted several years.

### 4. Moral Damages

In addition to the compensatory salary rights, the employee may request **moral damages** (daune morale) for the emotional distress, reputational harm, and psychological suffering caused by the unlawful dismissal. Courts have increasingly recognized moral damages in labor disputes, particularly in cases involving discriminatory or retaliatory dismissals.

### Alternative: Compensation Instead of Reinstatement

If the employee does not wish to be reinstated (for example, because the working relationship has been irreparably damaged), they may instead request compensation. Under **art. 80 para. 2**, the court will award compensation of at least **6 months' salary** and up to **24 months' salary**, in addition to the compensatory salary rights for the period between dismissal and the court's judgment.

## Documents to Request from Your Employer

If you have been dismissed or suspect that a dismissal is imminent, you should immediately request and secure the following documents:

- **The dismissal decision** (decizia de concediere) -- in original, with the employer's stamp and signature. This document is essential because the 45-day contest deadline runs from the date of notification.
- **Your individual employment contract** (contractul individual de munca) and all addenda (acte aditionale).
- **The internal regulations** (regulamentul intern) of the company.
- **The applicable collective labor agreement** (contractul colectiv de munca), if one exists.
- **Your job description** (fisa postului).
- **Performance evaluations** (evaluari profesionale), if any have been conducted.
- **Payslips** (fluturasi de salariu) for the last 12 months, to establish your salary level for the purpose of calculating compensatory rights.
- **The disciplinary hearing minutes** (procesul-verbal al cercetarii disciplinare prealabile), if the dismissal was for disciplinary reasons.
- **Medical certificates** or other documents demonstrating that you may have been in a protected category at the time of dismissal.

Under **art. 40 para. 2 lit. h of the Labor Code**, the employer is obligated to issue documents certifying the employee's work activity, seniority, and position, upon request.

## Signs of an Unlawful Dismissal

Be vigilant for the following indicators that your dismissal may be unlawful:

- **No written dismissal decision** was issued -- verbal dismissals have no legal effect in Romania.
- **The decision lacks mandatory content** -- it does not specify the grounds for dismissal, the evidence, or the deadline for contesting.
- **No prior disciplinary hearing** was conducted in the case of a disciplinary dismissal.
- **The notice period was not granted** when legally required.
- **The dismissal occurred during a protected period** -- such as medical leave, pregnancy, or parental leave.
- **The stated grounds appear pretextual** -- for example, a position is "eliminated" but a new employee is hired for an identical role shortly after.
- **The dismissal appears discriminatory or retaliatory** -- it followed the employee's filing of a complaint, reporting of irregularities, or exercise of a legal right (such as joining a union or taking parental leave).
