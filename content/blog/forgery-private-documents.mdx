## What is forgery in private documents?

Forgery in private documents is a criminal offense under **Article 322 of the Romanian Penal Code**. It consists of falsifying a private document by counterfeiting the writing or signature, or by altering it in any way, if the perpetrator uses the forged document or entrusts it to another person for use, with the purpose of producing a legal consequence.

The penalty provided by law is **imprisonment from 6 months to 3 years** or a **fine**.

This offense differs from forgery of official documents (Art. 320 Penal Code) through the nature of the falsified document. While official documents are issued by public authorities or institutions, private documents are those drawn up by natural or legal persons under private law, who do not hold the status of public official.

## Elements of the offense

For the offense of forgery in private documents under **Art. 322 para. 1 of the Penal Code** to exist, the following elements must be cumulatively met:

### The material object - the private document

A private document is any document drawn up and signed by private individuals, which records a legal act or a situation with legal relevance. Common examples include:

- **Contracts** - sale-purchase agreements, loan contracts, service agreements, lease contracts
- **Receipts and certificates** - documents attesting to the payment of a sum of money or a factual situation
- **Declarations** - statutory declarations, notarized declarations
- **Powers of attorney under private signature** - authorizations given by one person to another
- **Accounting documents** - invoices, delivery notes, commercial company accounting records
- **Holographic wills** - wills written, dated, and signed by the testator's own hand

### The material element - the act of forgery

Forgery can occur through two alternative methods:

**1. Counterfeiting** - creating a false document in its entirety, either by imitating another person's handwriting or by completely fabricating the document. Counterfeiting of the subscription means imitating or fabricating another person's signature.

**2. Alteration** - modifying a pre-existing authentic document through additions, deletions, word or number replacements, date changes, or any other intervention that changes the original content.

### Essential requirement - use of the document

A distinctive element of this offense compared to other forms of forgery is the requirement that the perpetrator must have **used the forged document** or **entrusted it to another person for use**. Simple forgery of a private document, without its use, does not constitute this offense.

This requirement reflects the rationale of criminalization: criminal law does not punish forgery per se, but rather the danger posed by putting a false document into circulation, which can generate legal consequences.

### Subjective element - intent

The offense is committed with **direct intent**. The perpetrator must act with the purpose of producing a legal consequence. It is not sufficient to merely know that the document is false - there must also be the will to use it for producing legal effects.

## Aggravated forms and connections with other offenses

### Use of forgery (Art. 323 Penal Code)

The use of a document forged by another person constitutes the offense of **use of forgery**, provided under **Art. 323 of the Penal Code**, and is punished with the same penalty as the forgery of the respective document.

### Concurrent offenses

Forgery in private documents may concur with other offenses, such as:

- **Fraud** (Art. 244 Penal Code) - when the forged document is used to deceive a person and obtain material benefit
- **Tax evasion** (Law No. 241/2005) - when accounting documents are forged to evade tax obligations
- **Computer forgery** (Art. 325 Penal Code) - when the forgery involves computer data
- **Breach of trust** (Art. 238 Penal Code) - when document forgery is connected to fraudulent disposition of entrusted property

## Statute of limitations

The limitation period for criminal liability for forgery in private documents is **5 years**, according to **Art. 154 para. 1 letter d) of the Penal Code** (for offenses punished with imprisonment of up to 5 years).

This period begins from the date the offense was committed, specifically from the date the forged document was used or entrusted to another person for use.

Under the **special limitation** (Art. 155 para. 4 Penal Code), criminal liability is prescribed regardless of interruption causes, within **10 years** (double the general limitation period).

## Favorable outcomes in judicial practice

### Case dismissal

In our firm's practice, we have obtained **dismissal decisions** for clients investigated for forgery in private documents. Dismissal is ordered by the prosecutor, pursuant to **Art. 315 of the Criminal Procedure Code**, when it is found that:

- **The act does not exist** - the document was not forged, the differences found having other explanations (material errors, modifications made by the document holder)
- **The act is not provided by criminal law** - the document does not meet the conditions of a private document under criminal law, or the requirement of use is missing
- **Insufficient evidence** - the evidence gathered during the criminal investigation does not support the accusation beyond reasonable doubt

In a specific case, our clients were investigated for forgery in private documents in the context of their professional activity. High workload and bureaucratic pressures generated suspicions regarding the authenticity of certain documents. Following the defenses formulated and the evidence administered during the criminal investigation phase, the prosecutor ordered the **dismissal of the case**, finding that the accusation of document forgery was not confirmed.

### Acquittal in court

In situations where the case reaches court, an acquittal may be pronounced under **Art. 396 para. 5 of the Criminal Procedure Code**, in relation to **Art. 16 para. 1** of the same code, when it is proven that:

- The act does not exist
- The act is not provided by criminal law
- There is no evidence that the defendant committed the offense
- There is a justificatory or non-imputable cause

## Effective defense strategies

In defending persons accused of forgery in private documents, a specialized attorney can employ several lines of defense:

### Contesting the constitutive elements

- **Lack of private document quality** - the document does not meet legal requirements to be considered a private document
- **Lack of document use** - the forged document was never used nor entrusted to another person, which excludes the existence of the offense
- **Lack of intent** - the perpetrator did not act with the intention of producing a legal consequence

### Expert examinations and technical evidence

- **Graphoscopic expertise** - can demonstrate that the writing or signature actually belongs to the indicated person, not to a forger
- **Technical document expertise** - technical analysis can reveal that apparent modifications have explanations other than intentional forgery
- **Documentary evidence** - presentation of original documents or other evidence contradicting the forgery accusation

### Mitigating circumstances

Even if the act exists, concrete circumstances may lead to a lighter sentence or alternative solutions:

- **Reduced or no actual damage**
- **Good conduct** of the defendant before and after the act
- **Circumstances of commission** - professional pressure, involuntary error transformed into criminal accusation
- **Plea agreement** (Art. 478-488 Criminal Procedure Code) - may lead to a one-third reduction in sentencing limits

## Frequently asked questions (FAQ)

### What documents are considered private documents?

Any document drawn up and signed by natural or legal persons under private law, recording rights, obligations, or situations with legal relevance: contracts, receipts, declarations, powers of attorney, invoices, certificates, holographic wills, and similar documents.

### What is the difference between forgery of official documents and forgery of private documents?

Forgery of official documents (Art. 320 Penal Code) concerns documents issued by public authorities or institutions and carries a heavier penalty (6 months to 5 years imprisonment). Forgery of private documents (Art. 322 Penal Code) concerns private documents and is punished with 6 months to 3 years imprisonment or a fine.

### If I signed a document on behalf of another person, with their consent, is it forgery?

The situation requires careful analysis. In principle, signing a document in another person's name, even with their verbal consent, may constitute forgery if it creates the appearance that the signature belongs to the other person. The existence of a written mandate or authorization may influence the legal classification.

### What happens if the forged document was never used?

If the forged document was not used and was not entrusted to another person for use, the act does not meet the constitutive elements of the offense of forgery in private documents under Art. 322 of the Penal Code.

### How long does a criminal trial for document forgery take?

Duration varies significantly depending on case complexity, number of parties involved, and volume of evidence. On average, the criminal investigation phase may last between 6 months and 2 years, while the first instance trial may take another 1-2 years. Appeal adds another 6-12 months.

### Can I obtain a suspended sentence?

Yes, in many cases. Given that the maximum penalty is 3 years imprisonment, the court may order supervised suspension of sentence execution (Art. 91-98 Penal Code) or postponement of sentence application (Art. 83-90 Penal Code), if legal conditions are met.
