## What is Medical Malpractice Under Romanian Law?

Medical malpractice, known in Romanian legislation as **malpraxis medical**, refers to a professional error committed by a healthcare provider -- whether a physician, nurse, pharmacist, or medical institution -- that results in harm to the patient. Under Romanian law, the primary legislative framework governing medical malpractice is **Legea nr. 95/2006 privind reforma in domeniul sanatatii** (Law no. 95/2006 on healthcare reform), specifically **Title XVI**, which addresses the civil liability of medical personnel and healthcare providers.

According to **art. 642 of Law no. 95/2006**, medical malpractice is defined as a professional error committed during the exercise of the medical act or in connection with it, causing harm to the patient, involving the civil liability of the medical personnel and the healthcare provider. This definition is broad enough to encompass a wide variety of situations, including but not limited to:

- **Diagnostic errors** -- failure to correctly identify a medical condition despite available clinical evidence, resulting in delayed or inappropriate treatment.
- **Surgical errors** -- mistakes made during surgical procedures, such as operating on the wrong site, leaving foreign objects inside the patient, or causing unnecessary tissue damage.
- **Medication errors** -- prescribing the wrong medication, incorrect dosages, or failing to account for known drug interactions and allergies.
- **Failure to obtain informed consent** -- performing a medical procedure without properly informing the patient of the risks and obtaining their explicit agreement.
- **Inadequate post-operative care** -- neglecting to monitor the patient after a procedure, failing to detect complications, or discharging the patient prematurely.
- **Hospital-acquired infections** -- where the healthcare facility fails to maintain adequate hygiene standards, leading to infections that could have been prevented.

**Art. 643-648 of Law no. 95/2006** further elaborate on the conditions under which civil liability arises, the role of expert medical commissions in establishing fault, and the relationship between individual practitioner liability and institutional liability. Importantly, **art. 644** specifies that civil liability can attach both to the individual medical professional and to the healthcare unit where the act was performed, creating the possibility of joint liability.

## Your Rights as a Patient

Romanian law provides patients with a comprehensive set of rights, codified primarily in **Legea nr. 46/2003 privind drepturile pacientului** (Law no. 46/2003 on patient rights). This law establishes fundamental protections that every patient is entitled to, regardless of the type of healthcare facility or the nature of the treatment. Understanding these rights is essential for anyone who believes they may have been a victim of medical malpractice.

### Right to Information (art. 4-8, Law no. 46/2003)

Every patient has the right to be informed about their state of health, the proposed medical interventions, the potential risks of treatment, and the available alternatives. This information must be provided in a manner that is understandable to the patient. If the healthcare provider fails to communicate this information clearly, any subsequent medical intervention may be considered as having been performed without proper consent.

### Right to Informed Consent (art. 13-17, Law no. 46/2003)

No medical procedure can be performed without the patient's informed consent, except in emergency situations where the patient is unable to express their will and there is no legal representative available. Consent must be given freely, after the patient has received all relevant information. For surgical interventions and procedures carrying significant risk, written consent is required.

### Right to a Second Medical Opinion (art. 10, Law no. 46/2003)

Patients have the right to request and receive a second opinion from another qualified medical professional. This right is particularly important in complex cases where the initial diagnosis or recommended treatment plan may be uncertain.

### Right to Confidentiality (art. 21-24, Law no. 46/2003)

All information related to a patient's health status, diagnosis, and treatment is confidential. Medical personnel are obligated to protect this information and may only disclose it with the patient's consent or in the specific circumstances permitted by law.

### Right to Compensation (art. 30-33, Law no. 46/2003)

In the event of medical malpractice, the patient has the right to seek compensation for the harm suffered. This right is further supported by the general provisions of the Romanian Civil Code regarding tort liability.

### Right to Access Medical Records (art. 18-20, Law no. 46/2003)

Patients have the right to access their complete medical records. Healthcare providers are obligated to maintain accurate records and to provide copies upon request. This right is crucial in building a malpractice case, as medical records constitute primary evidence.

## The Complaint Procedure: Step by Step

If you believe you have been the victim of medical malpractice in Romania, the law provides a structured procedure for seeking accountability and compensation. There are two main paths: an administrative complaint through a specialized medical commission, and a civil court action for damages.

### Step 1: Filing a Complaint with the Medical Monitoring Commission

The first step, often mandatory before initiating court proceedings, is to file a complaint with the **Comisia de monitorizare si competenta profesionala pentru cazurile de malpraxis** (Medical Monitoring and Professional Competence Commission for Malpractice Cases). These commissions operate at the level of each county public health directorate (**Directia de Sanatate Publica**).

According to **art. 679-681 of Law no. 95/2006**, the commission is composed of medical professionals and legal experts who examine the complaint and issue an expert opinion regarding whether malpractice occurred. The patient must submit:

- A written complaint describing the facts in detail
- All relevant medical documents (hospital discharge summaries, test results, prescriptions, imaging)
- Any correspondence with the healthcare provider

The commission's opinion, while not binding on the courts, carries significant weight as expert evidence in subsequent judicial proceedings.

### Step 2: Filing a Civil Court Action

If the commission's opinion confirms malpractice, or even if it does not (the patient retains the right to go to court regardless), the next step is filing a civil action for damages before the competent court. The claim is based on the general tort liability provisions of the Romanian Civil Code, specifically **art. 1349-1395**, combined with the special provisions of Law no. 95/2006.

The competent court is the **tribunal** (county-level court) in the jurisdiction where the medical act was performed or where the defendant has their domicile or headquarters.

### Step 3: Expert Medical Examination

During court proceedings, the judge will typically order a **forensic medical expertise** (expertiza medico-legala) through the **Institutul de Medicina Legala** (Institute of Forensic Medicine). This is a critical stage, as the forensic expert's report will establish whether the standard of care was breached and whether there is a causal link between the medical act and the harm suffered.

## Statute of Limitations

Understanding the time limits for filing a malpractice claim is absolutely critical. Under Romanian law, the general statute of limitations for personal injury claims, including medical malpractice, is **3 years** from the date the injured party became aware of the damage and the identity of the person responsible. This is established by **art. 2517 of the Codul Civil** (Civil Code), which provides the general three-year limitation period for actions in tort.

It is important to note that the statute of limitations begins to run not necessarily from the date of the medical act itself, but from the date the patient **discovered or should have discovered** the harm. In many malpractice cases, the effects of a medical error may not become apparent immediately. For example, a surgical instrument left inside the body may not cause symptoms for months or even years.

However, there is an absolute limitation period of **10 years** from the date the harm was caused, after which no claim can be brought regardless of when the patient discovered the injury (**art. 2528 Civil Code**).

Given these time constraints, it is essential to act promptly once you suspect malpractice. Delaying consultation with a lawyer can result in the permanent loss of your right to seek compensation.

## Types of Compensation

Romanian law provides for comprehensive compensation in medical malpractice cases, governed by the general provisions on civil liability found in **art. 1381-1393 of the Codul Civil** (Civil Code). The principle underlying compensation is **reparatio in integrum** -- the injured party should be restored, as far as possible, to the position they would have been in had the malpractice not occurred.

### Material Damages (Daune Materiale)

Material damages cover all quantifiable financial losses resulting from the malpractice. These may include:

- **Medical expenses** -- costs of corrective treatments, surgeries, medication, rehabilitation, and ongoing medical care necessitated by the malpractice.
- **Lost income** -- wages lost during the recovery period and, in severe cases, diminished future earning capacity.
- **Travel and accommodation costs** -- expenses incurred in seeking treatment, attending medical appointments, and traveling for expert evaluations.
- **Cost of assistance** -- if the malpractice resulted in a disability requiring home care or personal assistance.
- **Funeral expenses** -- in cases where the malpractice resulted in the patient's death, the family may claim funeral costs.

### Moral Damages (Daune Morale)

Under **art. 1391 of the Civil Code**, the injured party is entitled to compensation for non-material harm, including physical suffering, psychological distress, diminished quality of life, and aesthetic prejudice (such as scarring or disfigurement). The assessment of moral damages is at the court's discretion, taking into account the severity of the harm, its duration, and its impact on the victim's daily life.

In cases resulting in death, **art. 1391 para. 2** allows close family members to claim moral damages for the emotional suffering caused by the loss of their relative.

### Periodic Payments (Renta Viagera)

In cases involving permanent disability or long-term incapacity, the court may order periodic payments (**art. 1387 Civil Code**) to cover ongoing medical expenses and compensate for reduced earning capacity, rather than a single lump-sum award.

## Required Documentation

Building a strong malpractice case requires thorough documentation. Before and during the legal process, you should gather:

- **Complete medical records** from all healthcare providers involved, including hospital admission and discharge papers, surgical reports, anesthesia records, nursing notes, and daily observation charts.
- **Diagnostic imaging** -- X-rays, CT scans, MRI results, and ultrasound images.
- **Laboratory results** -- blood tests, pathology reports, and any other diagnostic tests performed.
- **Prescription records** -- all medications prescribed before, during, and after the incident.
- **Informed consent forms** -- the documents you signed (or were not asked to sign) prior to the medical intervention.
- **Second medical opinions** -- evaluations from other medical professionals regarding the standard of care you received.
- **Financial records** -- receipts, invoices, and proof of payment for all medical expenses, as well as documentation of lost income.
- **Photographs** -- visual evidence of injuries, surgical scars, or other physical effects of the malpractice.
- **Witness statements** -- accounts from family members or other individuals who were present during relevant medical interactions.

## Court Procedure and Burden of Proof

Medical malpractice litigation in Romania follows the general rules of civil procedure established by the **Codul de Procedura Civila** (Civil Procedure Code). However, there are several particularities that make these cases especially challenging.

### Burden of Proof

In principle, the burden of proof rests with the plaintiff (the patient). Under **art. 1349 of the Civil Code**, the patient must prove:

1. **The existence of a wrongful act** -- that the healthcare provider deviated from the accepted standard of medical care.
2. **The existence of harm** -- that the patient suffered quantifiable damage (physical, emotional, or financial).
3. **Causation** -- that there is a direct causal link between the wrongful act and the harm suffered.
4. **Fault** -- that the healthcare provider acted with negligence or intent.

In practice, establishing these elements requires specialized medical evidence, which is why the forensic medical expertise is so important. The forensic expert will evaluate whether the treatment provided met the standard of care expected of a reasonably competent practitioner in the same specialty, and whether a different course of action would have led to a different outcome.

### Duration of Proceedings

Medical malpractice cases in Romania can be lengthy, often lasting between **2 and 5 years** at first instance, depending on the complexity of the case, the number of expert evaluations required, and the court's caseload. Appeals can add an additional 1 to 2 years. This underscores the importance of having experienced legal representation to navigate the process efficiently.
